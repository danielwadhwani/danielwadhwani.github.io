Teaching

My courses are built to develop capacities, with imagination, judgment, and working with other people ahead of technique. I teach by the case method and write many of the cases myself.

Courses

At USC Marshall

  • GradFounder's DilemmasThe human side of founding: preparing to found, building the co-founding team, working with early hires and investors, and managing growth and exit. Also offered twice a year as a non-credit bootcamp.
  • UndergradEntrepreneurial ImaginationHow historical reasoning helps entrepreneurs imagine new opportunities, understand the institutions that shape resources, and win acceptance for something new.
  • GradVenture LabExamines venture as a learned craft. Students interview leading venture capitalists and take part in the practice of running a venture fund, USC Venture Capital, which is housed at the Greif Center.
  • GradEntrepreneurship through AcquisitionBuying and leading an existing business as a path into entrepreneurship, from raising capital and sourcing through diligence, closing, and exit. Students source live deals. See the USC Search Fund, a student initiative of the Greif Center.

Mentoring

Emerging scholars

I supervise doctoral students and postdoctoral scholars at USC and Copenhagen Business School. All four past postdocs at the Greif Center have gone on to tenure-track positions.

I have also taught doctoral workshops on historical approaches to entrepreneurship and management research at Exeter, Kyoto, Copenhagen Business School, and the Business History Conference.

Symposium

Historical Entrepreneurship Research Symposium

A small meeting at USC for scholars doing historical work on entrepreneurship, management, and organization. The fourth symposium runs January 29–30, 2027, on "The Moral and Political Foundations of Entrepreneurial Societies." It is co-organized with Christina Lubinski and Christian Garmann Johnsen of Copenhagen Business School.

Submissions are due December 15, 2026. Read the call for papers.
